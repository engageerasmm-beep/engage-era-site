Meta's Ad Engine Sees Lookalike Creatives as the Same Ad, Agencies Say

Swapping the caption or on-screen text isn't a new ad in Meta's eyes. Agencies say the system groups lookalike creative together. Here's what counts as genuinely different.

Ben Meller·Sep 29, 2026·4 min read
The short versionAccording to agencies quoted by Marketing Brew, Meta's ranking systems treat ads that share the same look or opening hook as essentially the same ad, even when the on-screen text changes. Meta calls creative diversification a longstanding best practice. If you want a real test, change the hook, the visual, the format or the message, not just the words.

If your "creative testing" on Meta means running the same video with three different captions, you may be testing less than you think.

What agencies are hearing

Agencies told Marketing Brew that Meta's engines won't rank an ad as highly if it has the same look or the same opening hook as an advertiser's other creative. Olivia Cripps, global paid media director at Billion Dollar Boy, said two videos of the same person with different on-screen text, "Text A" versus "Text B," would be considered the same creative.

Ankit Jadav of Rain the Growth Agency said Meta has been advising clients to mix video and static assets, and to vary messaging and tone through different hooks.

What Meta says

Meta spokesperson Alisha Swinteck called diversifying ad creative a "longstanding best practice as we see it deliver measurable results." Swinteck also said Meta has been testing creative insights metrics in its Insights API with a small number of agencies since June.

So this isn't a new rule. It's Meta being more explicit about how its system already sees your ads. Marketing Brew's reporting is the source here; we didn't find a separate official Meta announcement.

Why it matters

If the system treats five near-identical ads as one, you're not giving it five chances to find a winner. You're giving it one, split five ways. That can make a "test" look inconclusive when the real problem is that nothing was actually different.

What a real creative test looks like

  • Different hook: the first two seconds say or show something new.
  • Different visual: a new person, setting, product angle or style.
  • Different format: video vs. static vs. carousel.
  • Different message: price vs. quality vs. social proof vs. founder story.

What probably doesn't count: new caption, new on-screen text, new color filter on the same footage.

Sources

  • Marketing Brew: Meta's creative diversification guidance

FAQ

Does Meta treat similar ads as the same ad?

Agencies quoted by Marketing Brew say yes: ads with the same look or opening hook are treated as essentially the same creative, even if on-screen text differs.

Is this a new change from Meta?

Meta's spokesperson described creative diversification as a longstanding best practice. What's new is agencies hearing it spelled out more directly.

What counts as a different ad on Meta?

A different opening hook, a different visual or person, a different format (video vs. static), or a different message and tone.
