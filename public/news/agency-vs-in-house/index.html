Social Media Agency vs. In-House Hire: Which Is Right for Your Brand?

Hiring one person feels simpler. Hiring an agency feels bigger. Neither is automatically right. Here's how to decide based on what your brand actually needs.

Ben Meller·Sep 29, 2026·6 min read
The short versionAn in-house hire gives you one dedicated person who lives inside your brand. An agency gives you a team: strategy, content, editing, ads and reporting, without managing each role yourself. If you need one person to post consistently, hire. If you need strategy, production and growth together, an agency usually gets you further, faster.

Almost every growing brand hits the same question: do we hire a social media person, or bring in an agency?

The honest answer is that it depends on what you're actually missing. Here's how to think it through.

What you get with an in-house hire

  • Someone inside the brand every day. They're in the meetings, in the store, at the events. Nobody knows the day-to-day better.
  • Speed on small things. A quick story, a reply, a post about today's news. No brief needed.
  • Full control. You set their priorities and their schedule.

The catch: you're asking one person to be a strategist, photographer, editor, copywriter, community manager, ad buyer and analyst. Very few people are great at all of those. You also take on hiring, training, managing, and covering for them when they're out or when they leave.

What you get with an agency

  • A team, not a person. Strategy, content production, editing, paid social and reporting from people who each specialize.
  • Pattern recognition. An agency sees what's working across many accounts and platforms right now, not just yours.
  • Less management. You approve the plan and the work instead of directing every task.

The catch: an agency isn't in your building. The best partnerships need a clear point of contact on your side, fast approvals, and access to your world, whether that's events, product, or the founder.

The real cost comparison

Don't compare a salary to an agency retainer line by line. Compare the full cost of getting the result you want:

  • Salary plus benefits, payroll taxes and tools for a hire
  • The time you or your team spend managing and training
  • The skills one person can't cover, and what you'd pay freelancers to fill those gaps
  • How long it takes to see results while someone ramps up

Often the question isn't "which is cheaper," it's "which actually gets us where we're trying to go."

A simple way to decide

Hire in-house if:

  • You already know your strategy and mostly need consistent posting
  • Content depends on being on-site every day
  • You have someone with the time to manage them

Work with an agency if:

  • You need strategy, production and growth, not just posting
  • You want a founder brand, launches or paid social done well
  • You'd rather approve work than manage a department

Use both if: you want day-to-day presence from someone inside, plus strategy and production firepower from outside. That combination is common with brands that are scaling.

Questions to ask before you choose

  1. What result do we want in 90 days: followers, leads, sales, reputation?
  2. Who on our side will own approvals and access?
  3. Do we need someone to post, or someone to decide what's worth posting?
  4. What would we do if our one hire left next month?

Answer those honestly and the choice usually makes itself.

FAQ

Is an agency more expensive than hiring in-house?

Not always. A single hire comes with salary, benefits, tools and management time, and one person rarely covers strategy, shooting, editing, ads and analytics equally well. Compare the total cost of the result you want, not just the monthly number.

When should a brand hire in-house instead?

When you need someone on-site every day, when content depends on constant access to your team or location, or when you already have strategy and just need consistent execution.

Can you use both?

Yes. Many brands keep one in-house person for day-to-day posting and community, and use an agency for strategy, production and campaigns.
